Join and transform polygons

Real spatial work when the polygons start out in the table rather than in R

The spatial functions are on both sides of the line

Every one of the 23 ST_ functions probed for this guide works server-side, so needing a spatial operation is not by itself a reason to bring data back. Buffers, intersections, transforms, areas and predicates all run where the data is.

That matters because the obvious chain of reasoning is wrong. Spatial work means sf, sf means R, R means collecting everything first: each step follows from the last and the conclusion is that geometry always crosses. It does not. You can put a spatial question to the server and get back a number.

Here is the shape, on the catchment polygons used throughout this guide. Four thousand polygons are read, unioned and measured, and one row comes back. Two connections are open because this page needs both: con runs the spatial SQL, and acon is the one that can carry raw geometry back, for the reason Connect your R session to the data sets out.

library(DBI)
library(dplyr)
library(dbplyr)
library(tibble)
library(glue)
library(cli)
library(sf)

catalog <- Sys.getenv("DATABRICKS_CATALOG")
schema <- Sys.getenv("DATABRICKS_SCHEMA")

con <- dbConnect(
  odbc::databricks(),
  httpPath = Sys.getenv("DATABRICKS_PATH"),
  DefaultStringColumnLength = 65535
)

acon <- dbConnect(
  brickster::DatabricksSQL(),
  warehouse_id = basename(Sys.getenv("DATABRICKS_PATH"))
)

tbl_name <- function(table) {
  glue("{catalog}.{schema}.{table}")
}

dbGetQuery(con, glue(
  "SELECT count(*) AS n_catchments,
          round(sum(st_area(g)) / 1e6) AS area_km2
   FROM (SELECT st_setsrid(st_geomfromwkb(geom_wkb), 27700) AS g
         FROM {tbl_name('wfd_catchments')}
         WHERE geom_wkb IS NOT NULL)"
))
# A tibble: 1 × 2
  n_catchments area_km2
       <int64>    <dbl>
1         4080   130229

One caveat before you rely on that count. It was measured on a Pro warehouse running serverless, which is the kind of compute the spatial functions are documented to need. On the older Classic warehouse type they are documented as unavailable, and a coverage number means nothing without saying which warehouse it came from. If a spatial query of yours fails with an unknown-function error, the most likely explanation is the warehouse you were given rather than the SQL you wrote, and nothing in the error will say so. That is a question for whoever provisioned the compute.

Set the CRS yourself

The polygons are stored as WKB in a BINARY column, and WKB carries no SRID. Ask the server what coordinate system the stored bytes are in and it says zero, meaning it does not know:

dbGetQuery(con, glue(
  "SELECT water_body_id,
          st_srid(st_geomfromwkb(geom_wkb)) AS srid
   FROM {tbl_name('wfd_catchments')}
   WHERE geom_wkb IS NOT NULL
   LIMIT 3"
))
# A tibble: 3 × 2
  water_body_id   srid
  <chr>          <int>
1 GB105035046170     0
2 GB109055036620     0
3 GB112069061390     0

The number has to come from you. For this data it is EPSG:27700, the British National Grid, because that is what the Environment Agency publishes. st_setsrid() asserts it server-side and st_set_crs() asserts it in R. Neither reprojects anything: they attach a label to coordinates that already had that meaning.

Skip it and the two sides fail differently, which is the part worth knowing before you pick where to work. R computes anyway. Decode a polygon, ask for its area without declaring anything, and you get numbers with no CRS, no warning and nothing wrong-looking about them:

rows <- dbGetQuery(acon, glue(
  "SELECT geom_wkb FROM {tbl_name('wfd_catchments')}
   WHERE geom_wkb IS NOT NULL LIMIT 3"
))

geom <- st_as_sfc(structure(rows$geom_wkb, class = "WKB"), EWKB = TRUE)

st_crs(geom)$epsg
as.numeric(st_area(geom))
[1] NA
[1] 25807299 15914043 19662869

Those areas happen to be right, because the stored coordinates are already metres. Feed the same CRS-less geometry to anything that needs to know where on Earth it is, though, and the silence ends: a distance in degrees, a join against a layer in another projection, a transform. The answer is wrong or absent and nothing says which.

The server is stricter, and its strictness is a gift here. It refuses rather than guessing:

! ODBC failed with error 00000 from
  [RStudio][Hardy][ST_INVALID_CRS_TRANSFORMATION_ERROR].
✖  (35) Error from server: error code: '0' error message:
  'org.apache.spark.SparkIllegalArgumentException: st_transform: Invalid
  or unsupported CRS transformation from SRI'.

So the habit to build is to wrap st_geomfromwkb() in st_setsrid() every time, in the innermost subquery, and never let an un-labelled geometry out of it.

Transforming once, server-side, instead of per iteration

st_transform() works server-side, so a reprojection can happen once, in the query, rather than in R on every pass of an analysis. The storm overflow locations are longitude and latitude, EPSG:4326, and the catchments are EPSG:27700, so something has to move before the two layers can meet:

dbGetQuery(con, glue(
  "SELECT overflow_id,
          round(st_x(p), 1) AS easting,
          round(st_y(p), 1) AS northing
   FROM (SELECT overflow_id,
                st_transform(st_point(longitude, latitude, 4326),
                             27700) AS p
         FROM {tbl_name('storm_overflows')}
         WHERE longitude IS NOT NULL
         LIMIT 3)"
))
# A tibble: 3 × 3
  overflow_id easting northing
  <chr>         <dbl>    <dbl>
1 SVT00708    384585.  205042.
2 SVT00753    434509.  347118.
3 SVT01386    388700.  250259.

Note that st_point() takes the SRID as its third argument, which is what makes the transform legal. The same rule as above: say what the coordinates are before asking anything to move them.

Aggregating server-side when only a scalar needs to cross

This is the best case on the site, and the shape to look for in your own analysis: the geometry stays where it is, the spatial work happens there, and a small table comes back.

Group by an ordinary column while aggregating the geometry. Here that is ecological class, six values, with the catchments in each class unioned and the union’s area measured:

dbGetQuery(con, glue(
  "SELECT ecological_class,
          count(*) AS n,
          round(st_area(st_union_agg(g)) / 1e6) AS area_km2
   FROM (SELECT ecological_class,
                st_setsrid(st_geomfromwkb(geom_wkb), 27700) AS g
         FROM {tbl_name('wfd_catchments')}
         WHERE geom_wkb IS NOT NULL
           AND ecological_class IS NOT NULL)
   GROUP BY ecological_class
   ORDER BY area_km2 DESC"
))
# A tibble: 6 × 3
  ecological_class       n area_km2
  <chr>            <int64>    <dbl>
1 Moderate            2208    74367
2 Poor                 710    23266
3 Good                 512    14089
4 Not assessed         219     4831
5 Bad                  119     4044
6 High                   4       76

Six rows crossed. The 27 MB of polygons behind them did not.

You may have read that geometry cannot take part in grouped queries. The restriction is much narrower than that reads. Grouping by an ordinary key while aggregating geometry works, as above, and so does grouping by something derived from the geometry such as a geohash. What fails is grouping by a geometry column itself, because the type has no ordering. The error says exactly that and names the column:

✖  (80) Syntax or semantic analysis error thrown in server while
  executing query. Error message from server:
  org.apache.spark.sql.catalyst.ExtendedAnalysisException: The
  expression "g" cannot be used as a grouping e

So the pattern is not blocked, only one spelling of it is, and the one you want is the spelling that works.

The full sf pipeline below the line, when that is what you want

Bringing the polygons back is a legitimate answer, not a defeat. If the geometry fits in your session and you have an sf pipeline already written, collecting it is the right call: you get the whole of sf rather than the subset the server exposes, and you are working in an idiom you already know.

The connection caveat decides how. Geometry cannot travel over the ODBC path, because odbc silently returns only part of a BINARY value, so this route needs brickster::DatabricksSQL(). See Connect your R session to the data for why, and for the check that the bytes arrived whole.

catchments <- dbGetQuery(acon, glue(
  "SELECT water_body_id, ecological_class, water_body_area_m2, geom_wkb
   FROM {tbl_name('wfd_catchments')}
   WHERE geom_wkb IS NOT NULL"
)) |>
  as_tibble() |>
  mutate(
    geometry = st_as_sfc(structure(geom_wkb, class = "WKB"),
                         EWKB = TRUE) |>
      st_set_crs(27700)
  ) |>
  select(-geom_wkb) |>
  st_as_sf()

All 4,080 polygons, 27.2 MB in the session, and the projection named in the header rather than left blank. That is what a good outcome looks like: Projected CRS: OSGB36 / British National Grid is the line to check before going any further, because the alternative is a blank where it should be and no other symptom.

Twenty-seven megabytes is small, and the honest reading of that is that this dataset never needed a decision. If your polygons are of that order and nobody is dictating where the analysis lives, your laptop will do this faster than any of the above and you should not think about it again. The server-side route earns its complexity when the geometry is large and the answer is small, not when both are small.

Points work the same way

Nothing above is specific to polygons. Points get the same treatment, and the predicate join runs server-side too. Counting storm overflows inside catchments, grouped by the catchment’s ecological class, takes both layers, transforms one of them and returns five rows:

dbGetQuery(con, glue(
  "SELECT c.ecological_class, count(*) AS n_overflows
   FROM {tbl_name('wfd_catchments')} c
   JOIN {tbl_name('storm_overflows')} o
     ON st_contains(
          st_setsrid(st_geomfromwkb(c.geom_wkb), 27700),
          st_transform(st_point(o.longitude, o.latitude, 4326),
                       27700))
   WHERE c.ecological_class IS NOT NULL
   GROUP BY c.ecological_class
   ORDER BY n_overflows DESC"
))
# A tibble: 5 × 2
  ecological_class n_overflows
  <chr>                <int64>
1 Moderate                8491
2 Poor                    2027
3 Good                     547
4 Not assessed             436
5 Bad                      276

Both SRIDs are asserted inside the join condition, and that is not decoration: st_contains() refuses two arguments with different SRIDs rather than assuming they match. Reading the overflow data here is subject to its own licence, which is CC BY 4.0 and not the Open Government Licence that covers the rest.

The worked example does this same join the other way, in sf after collecting both layers, and then checks the two answers against each other. See The spatial join if you want the comparison rather than the recipe.
